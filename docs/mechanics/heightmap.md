# Heightmap terrain

Natural ground for the battlefield level: heights on a regular sample grid, drawn and collided in chunks, read by the map grid straight from the data (`docs/plans/battlefield.md`, step 2).

**Scripts:** `terrain/terrain_heights.gd` (data), `terrain_chunks.gd` (the node), `terrain_chunk_mesh.gd` (facet mesh: `arrays()` pure, `make_mesh()` main thread), `terrain_light.gd` (baked vertex colours), `terrain_shadow.gd` + `terrain_shadow.gdshaderinc` (sun and moon shadow), `ground.gdshader` (the ground's material), `terrain_test_map.gd` (generators), `heightmap_png.gd` (16-bit PNG codec), `heightmap_import.gd` (PNG <-> `TerrainHeights`), `terrain/terrain_brush.gd` (brush maths), `debug/heightmap_io/` (`heightmap_reloader.gd`, `heightmap_brush.gd`, `height_bands.gd` + shader), `debug/day_night/day_night_toggle.gd`; `map/map_grid.gd` (`terrain`); test level `levels/heightmap_test/` (`HeightmapTestLevel`, not maintained).

## How it works
- **`TerrainHeights`** (`Resource`, no nodes): `origin`, `spacing` (metres between samples, coarser than the map grid's cell), `samples`, 16-bit heights over `min_height` + `height_range` in a byte array, a painted role byte per sample (0 = pick by slope and height). Seabed is in the same data: below `MapGrid.SEA_LEVEL` is water depth. `height_at(x, z)` blends four samples; `set_sample_height` changes one and emits nothing. Reads go through a copy decoded to metres (made on first read, not thread-safe: `prepare()` before threads read), updated by `set_index_height` and dropped when `data`, `min_height` or `height_range` is assigned, so never write into `data` in place.
- **`TerrainChunks`** (`setup(data)`): the map cut into chunks of `chunk_cells` cells (corner samples shared). Per chunk a near mesh and a coarse mesh (every `lod_step`-th sample), switched by visibility range at `lod_distance`, and a `HeightMapShape3D` on the World layer (shape node scaled to the spacing), all under one `StaticBody3D`. Meshes are flat-shaded facets, one surface per palette role (`WorldMaterials.ground()`, a shader material that shades like the models'); the role is the painted one or picked from slope (stone), height (sand at the shore, soil on the summit, grass between) and depth (seabed: sand shallow, dark stone deep).
- **Submerged chunks:** after `set_water_cover(area, level)`, a chunk inside `area` whose highest sample is more than `submerged_depth` under `level` gets no meshes (collision stays). The atoll passes the sea's rectangle and its deepest wave trough, before `setup` so they are never built, and again when the water changes. An edit or a live tuning change gives or takes the meshes. No cover (the test level): every chunk is drawn.
- **Changes:** after editing samples, call `changed(area)`. Only the chunks the area touches queue; `chunks_per_tick` rebuild per physics tick; when the queue empties `changed_area(area)` fires. A level connects it to `MapGrid.resample`, which re-reads the data (no wait for physics) and emits `cells_changed`, so units re-path as for any map change.
- **`MapGrid.terrain`:** when set, cells take their height from the data (cells below sea level count as no ground, so the grid stays land only). Unset: rays at colliders.

## Baked light (battlefield step 10b-4)
`TerrainLight` bakes one colour multiplier per sample into the chunk vertex colours: cavity occlusion (neighbours at `light_ao_radius` against the sample) and a low-frequency warm/cool tint (field patchwork). Free per frame in both views. The level gives the sun with `TerrainChunks.set_sun` before `setup`; without it the ground stays white and unshadowed. Tunables: `HeightmapStats` "Baked light". Water-covered samples (below the sea by more than 1 m) are left white.
- **Edits:** `changed(area)` re-bakes `TerrainLight.affected(area)`, the area grown by the cavity radius, and rebuilds the chunks over it.
- **Settings:** a change to the hollow or tint settings re-bakes everything and queues every chunk.

## Terrain shadow (match flow step 3a)
The sun's or moon's horizon shadow is a **mask**, not vertex colour, so it blocks only the directional light: ambient fill, searchlights and other lamps light shadowed ground like any other. View code only; detection and the simulation never read it, and units get no terrain shadow (real-time shadow maps are separate and unchanged).
- **`TerrainShadow`:** one byte per height sample (255 lit), traced by a sweep away from the sun, soft over `light_shadow_softness` metres of height. The sea receives at its own level, so shadows lie on water. It goes to the shaders as an R8 texture through global uniforms (`terrain_shadow.gdshaderinc`); a level without a mask is all lit.
- **Who reads it:** `ground.gdshader` (the chunks) and `water.gdshader`, which also darkens the sea's self-lit body colour, or the shadow hardly shows. A custom `light()` is Godot's only way to block one kind of light, so the ground repeats the standard diffuse and highlight there. Overlays (clouds, height bands) draw over it as before.
- **Strength:** per light preset, `LightStats.terrain_shadow` (share of directional light blocked) and `terrain_shadow_sea` (the sea's glow). Live, no re-trace.
- **Reach:** the longest shadow is the highest ground over the sun's slope, with no cap. The peak only grows, so cutting a hill still re-traces as far as its old shadow.
- **Edits:** `changed(area)` re-traces everything downwind of the area (`TerrainShadow.affected`) and uploads the texture. No chunk rebuilds for a shadow.
- **A new sun direction or softness** (the night toggle, live tuning) re-traces the whole map on the main thread, about half a second per step of a dragged slider.
- **Night:** the atoll loads `light_night.tres`. The moon is low in the south, across the west-east team axis, so shadows fall mirrored for the two teams, and behind the default camera, so units and hulls face it lit. From the north it gave stronger shadows toward the camera but dark units and a large glitter patch in view. N (`debug_day_night`, `DayNightToggle`) swaps day and night. The sea, bird's-eye view and sky follow the preset too: `LightStats` Sea and clouds group (day is 1) and the `WORLD_NIGHT_*` colours (`water.md`, `birds_eye.md`). A night sky is `lighting/night_sky.gdshader`: the day sky's gradient curves plus procedural stars (`LightStats` Sky group), sized in screen pixels, with no `TIME` so the sky isn't re-baked; only the cockpit sees them.

## Authoring (battlefield step 3a)
Sculpt outside the game (Krita or Blender), save a 16-bit greyscale PNG at `HeightmapStats.source_path` (Import group); the game re-imports it within `reload_poll_seconds` and rebuilds only the chunks whose samples changed.
- **Encoding:** black is `min_height`, white is `min_height + height_range` (defaults -50 m and +150 m, so sea level 0 m is grey 25%). One pixel is one sample, `import_spacing` apart (default 4 m), the map centred on the origin, top row north (-z). Surface roles are not in the image; slope and height pick them.
- **Krita:** new image, greyscale, 16-bit integer, a linear or "no colour conversion" profile (a gamma curve bends the heights), no alpha needed. Export as PNG without interlacing, keeping 16 bits. Our reader takes greyscale, grey + alpha, RGB or RGBA at 8 or 16 bits (first channel); 8-bit works but steps the ground in 0.8 m. Blender: bake the height to a 16-bit PNG, same rules.
- **How to paint** (brush setup, exact heights, ground rules): `docs/terrain_painting.md`.
- **Starter:** if the source PNG is missing, the atoll writes a generated draft there (`AtollBlockout`); open it in Krita and paint over it.
- **Keys (`HeightmapReloader`):** `debug_heightmap_reload` (F6) re-imports now; `debug_heightmap_save` (F7) writes the current heights, in-game edits included, back to the PNG.
- **Why our own codec:** `Image.save_png` writes 8 bits and the loader drops to 8 bits. `HeightmapPng` writes greyscale 16-bit (zlib via `PackedByteArray.compress`) and reads all five row filters.
- **Brushes (3b, `HeightmapBrush`, `TerrainBrush`):** # turns the brush on (the left button then paints instead of selecting); + cycles raise, lower, flatten (to the height first clicked) and crater (one dig per click, small rim); `,` and `.` change the radius. Rates, radius and crater depth are in `HeightmapStats` (Brush group). Edits call `TerrainChunks.changed()`, so the grid re-reads heights and units re-path. No undo: F6 re-imports the PNG; F7 saves brush work back.
- **Height bands (3b, `HeightBands`):** 9 overlays height colours, a contour every `contour_interval` metres and an orange waterline (a material overlay on every chunk view through `TerrainChunks.set_overlay`).
- **Tab page:** `ui/keybinds/keybind_page.gd` (autoload `KeybindPage`) lists game keys left and debug / map-design keys right, read from the Input Map; add a row to its two arrays when adding an action.
- **Not yet:** the Krita reference layer, the far camera (3c, 3d in the plan). The atoll level (`levels/atoll/`) uses it since battlefield step 5.

## Reference layer (battlefield step 3d)
`debug_reference_layer` (0, in `HeightmapReloader`) writes `reference_layer.png` next to the heightmap PNG from `ReferenceLayer` (`debug/heightmap_io/reference_layer.gd`): a transparent PNG with one pixel per heightmap sample, to open in Krita as a layer above the painting and never export with it. It holds a labelled grid every 100 m (stronger every 500 m), the heightmap's edge and the battlefield's `map_extent` in red, a 500 m scale bar, the atoll ring radius (orange), the two mothership footprints at their hover position (purple), a one-unit dot and a 100-unit squad block (green), and a ruler of grey patches with their percent and height, sea level marked in blue. Ring, mothership and extent come from `BattlefieldStats`, the ruler from the heightmap's range; regenerate when they change. Its tiny 3 x 5 font draws capitals and digits only. The pixel size is the loaded heightmap's, so match the painting to it.

## Measured
Terrain shadow (headless M1, atoll 876 x 501, night moon, reach about 530 m): full trace about 0.47 s (on the load's ground thread; on the main thread for a toggle, whole swap about 0.48 s). An edit's trace and upload: 40 m square about 4 ms, 200 m about 14 ms with the sun along an axis; a diagonal sun about 33 and 47 ms. A 200 m edit's light and shadow together took 18 ms, with its chunks rebuilt over 3 ticks. Atoll level added to ready about 3.7 s (not compared with the same build without the mask). Checked: an edited, undone and peak-cut mask matches a full trace at four sun directions; a spot light gives the same pixel with the mask on or off.

Atoll load (headless M1, rough): the sea covers 328 of 448 chunks; level added to playable about 3.1 s threaded, 4.7 s serial (`battlefield.md`, "Load"; 10.9 to 16.1 s before the height cache, submerged chunks and tighter grid and shore loops). Drawn chunks' meshes, light bake and PNG decode about 0.9 s each. Threading: `docs/plans/threading.md`.

Test level (640 x 640 m, 2 m samples, 321 x 321 samples, 10 x 10 chunks of 32 cells), headless M1: data 301 KB; level load 1.7 s including grid and units; collision ray hits match `height_at` to 0.00 m; 8 units pathed across the island and arrived; raising a 12 m square rebuilt 2 chunks (it straddled a border) and the grid height followed.

Import (headless): a hand-built PNG using all five row filters reads back exactly; a written PNG round-trips and passes CRC checks; a 876 x 501 map (3.5 x 2 km at 4 m) writes in about 40 ms and reads in about 50 ms (flat data, real paintings compress less); reloading an edit of 6 x 6 samples took 6 ms and rebuilt one chunk in one tick.

## Known issues
- Water does not follow the heights (a `WaterBody` is a flat sheet at its node's height; below-sea ground only shows through it in levels that have one; step 4 derives shores). The test level's generator is a stand-in.
- Auto-reload refuses a PNG of a different size or spacing (restart the level); a half-written file is skipped by the next save's modified time.
- Water does not use the heights yet (step 4); the test level has a flat `WaterBody` at 0 m (no shore foam), because without it the sea floor (dark stone and sand) reads as flat grey.
- The mesh's quad split and Jolt's heightmap triangulation may differ on a diagonal, a few centimetres on steep ground.
- Not tested: a path crossing a changed chunk re-pathing (the generic map-change re-path covers it); LOD seams between a near and a far chunk (small cracks possible).
